Learning Competency 1: Roles in a Food Web

TOS Weight: 33% | Items 1–6

1. Producers (Autotrophs)

Organisms that produce their own food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide through Photosynthesis. They form the foundation of all food webs.

Examples: Mango Trees, Corn Plants, Green Algae, Rice Plants.

2. Consumers (Heterotrophs)

Organisms that cannot make their own food and must consume other organisms for energy.

  • Herbivores (Primary): Eat producers (e.g., Caterpillars, Grasshoppers).
  • Carnivores (Secondary/Tertiary): Eat other animals (e.g., Frogs, Snakes, Eagles).
  • Omnivores: Eat both plants and animals (e.g., Chickens, Humans).

3. Scavengers

Animals that feed on dead bodies of organisms that they did not kill themselves. They clean up dead matter in the environment.

Examples: Vultures, Crows, Hyenas, Crows in farm fields.

4. Decomposers

Organisms that chemically break down dead organic tissue and recycle vital minerals and nutrients back into the soil for plants to use again.

Examples: Mushrooms (Fungi), Bacteria, Earthworms.

Learning Competency 2: Ecological Interactions

TOS Weight: 33% | Items 7–13

Mutualism (+ / +)

A relationship where both organisms benefit from each other.

🐝 Bee & Flower: Bee gets nectar; flower gets pollinated!

Commensalism (+ / 0)

One organism benefits, while the other is neither helped nor harmed.

🌿 Orchid on Tree: Orchid gets high sunlight; tree is unharmed!

Parasitism (+ / -)

One organism (parasite) benefits by living on/in a host and causing harm.

🦟 Mosquito & Human: Mosquito gets blood; human suffers itch/disease!

Predation

An interaction where one organism (predator) hunts, kills, and eats another organism (prey).

🦅 Eagle & Flying Fox: Eagle hunts flying fox for food.

Competition

The struggle between organisms for limited resources like sunlight, water, food, or space.

🌾 Weeds & Rice Plants: Weeds steal soil minerals and light from rice.

Learning Competency 3: Biotic & Abiotic Factors

TOS Weight: 33% | Items 14–20

Biotic Factors

All the living organisms (or once-living matter) in an ecosystem.

  • 🌱 Green Plants, Rice Crops, Trees
  • 🐸 Frogs, Caterpillars, Snakes, Fish
  • 🍄 Mushrooms, Soil Bacteria, Earthworms

Abiotic Factors

All the non-living physical and chemical components of an ecosystem.

  • ☀️ Light: Source of energy for photosynthesis.
  • 💧 Water: Essential for chemical processes & survival.
  • 🌡️ Temperature: Determines organism activity & climate limits.
  • 🪴 Soil Type: Provides nutrients & support (Loam, Clay, Sand).
